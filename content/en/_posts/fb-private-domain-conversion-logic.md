> **TL;DR:** Chat-led conversion suits purchases that require explanation, trust or negotiation. A self-service store suits standardized products and shorter decisions. Choose the model around the product and customer.

## How the models differ

Neither approach is inherently more professional. They rely on different ways of helping a customer decide.

| Dimension | Chat-led conversion | Online-store conversion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main mechanism | Build trust through conversation | Explain and persuade through the page |
| Typical products | High-value, B2B or customized items | Standardized, easily understood items |
| Path | Ad → WhatsApp/Messenger → conversation → order | Ad → landing page → checkout |
| Core capability | Expertise, communication and response speed | Page design, payments and conversion flow |

## 1. Products suited to conversations

### Adult-product categories

The original article identifies privacy, product-specific questions and the need for one-to-one communication as reasons customers may prefer a private conversation.

Its workflow describes informational page content, contextual creative, a page or Messenger destination and a WhatsApp business account.

### Jade, jewelry and luxury products

These products can require explanation of material, color, workmanship and customization. The proposed approach combines professional presentation, clear photographs, certificates and individual support.

### Factories and wholesale suppliers

Business buyers need to discuss price, customization, lead times and payment terms. They also want evidence of production capability.

The original workflow uses real factory footage, samples of bulk orders and customer cases to support those discussions.

## 2. Run a useful sales conversation

### Retail and personal customization

**Start with the need.** Ask about the intended occasion, features and specifications instead of simply asking what the customer wants to buy.

**Narrow the choice.** Recommend two or three relevant options, each with a clear selling point and actual product photographs.

**Explain price and terms.** The original example starts with the base price, then describes quantity discounts, shipping and availability.

**Explain after-sales service.** Make delivery tracking, returns and damage-replacement arrangements clear.

### Wholesale, private-label and customized orders

1. **Identify the buyer and requirement.** Establish whether they are a wholesaler, retailer or trading company, then discuss quantity, minimum order, customization and timing.
2. **Demonstrate capability.** Share factory and bulk-order evidence, production capacity and relevant export capabilities before presenting the quotation.
3. **Quote in tiers.** The original example gives prices for 50 and 100 units, with private-label terms discussed at 500 units.
4. **Confirm the agreement in writing.** Record quantity, unit price, production time, payment, logistics and after-sales terms before collecting a deposit.

### Practices shared by both

| Practice | What to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| Response time | Coverage during the customer's working hours |
| Quick replies | Answers about price, delivery, after-sales service and minimum orders |
| Follow-up | Relevant updates for customers still considering the offer |
| Existing customers | A clear contact channel and repeat-order arrangements |

The original article suggests following up after three to five days, with relevant new arrivals or an offer.

## 3. Products suited to an online store

Standardized apparel, beauty products, accessories, bags and watches can be easier to sell through a clear product page and self-service checkout.

The proposed process is:

1. Use a sales campaign objective.
2. Measure the path with a pixel and conversion API.
3. Move from creative to landing page to checkout.

Poor images, an unclear page or a cumbersome payment flow can stop the process. Product information, relevant credentials and a smooth checkout support confidence.

## 4. Choose the model

The original article uses the following decision prompts:

- Is the order value at least $500? Consider whether personal discussion is needed.
- Is the product standardized, with a clear SKU? A store may be suitable.
- Is the buyer a business with a long purchasing process? Conversation is likely to matter.
- Does the customer need to speak with a real person to feel comfortable? Provide that route.

These prompts are a framework for evaluating the purchase, rather than a claim that one model always wins.

| Model | Suitable characteristics | Main skills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chat-led | High value, B2B, customization and trust-building | Product expertise, communication and support |
| Store-led | Standardization and a short decision process | Creative, page design and checkout |

The original methodology covers jade, jewelry, adult-product categories, factories and other cross-border businesses.

Geographic Targeting for High-Value Products

Created2026-04-02|Updated2026-04-02|Global Growth
|Post Views:
English edition, translated and edited from the Chinese article. Figures and product details refer to the original publication date. 中文原文

TL;DR: The original article proposes narrowing geographic targeting when selling expensive products. The workflow is to research relevant areas, validate their postal codes where applicable, and test them in the advertising platform.

Why geography matters

Jade, jewelry, luxury goods and customized products can attract many inquiries that do not become orders. The original article suggests investigating whether the selected areas match the intended market.

Purchasing patterns differ within a country or city. Geographic segmentation is one way to test a market hypothesis, alongside the product, offer and creative.

Research and validate the locations

The original workflow starts with searches for relevant residential or commercial areas in the target market, then organizes the results for import.

Correction to the original example: Hong Kong does not use a postcode system. For Hong Kong, research named districts or other geographic options supported by the advertising platform; do not ask an AI tool to invent local postal codes. UPU Addressing and Postcode Manual, Hong Kong case study

For markets that use postal codes, check the code against the local postal service and confirm what the advertising platform actually matches. A search or AI-generated list is a starting point for verification.

Configure geographic targeting

  1. Open the campaign’s ad-set settings.
  2. Find the location controls.
  3. Use the bulk-location workflow if it is available.
  4. Select the supported location type and target country.
  5. Paste the verified locations.
  6. Review every matched location before adding it.

The original article’s Kuala Lumpur code list is not reproduced as a verified location mapping. Confirm each area’s code and platform match before using it.

Combine geography with other signals

The original article proposes combining geographic tests with product-related interests, shopping behavior and location exclusions.

Examples of product interests in the article include jade, jewelry, collecting, auctions, art and gifts. Available targeting controls need to be checked in the account in use.

Examples from the original article

Product Market Area-selection approach
Jade Malaysia Research and verify relevant areas
Jade Singapore Check the target area and postal-code match
Jade Hong Kong Use supported named geographic areas
Luxury products California Verify the intended ZIP-code areas
Wholesale Multiple markets Research industrial and commercial centers

The original article reported campaign-improvement percentages without a linked dataset. They are not reproduced here as verified benchmarks.

Common mistakes

Treating a whole city as one audience

A city-level audience may combine very different markets. Smaller geographic tests can help distinguish their performance.

Combining dissimilar markets in one ad set

The article advises separating markets such as the United States, Malaysia and Singapore when their costs, language and demand differ.

Ignoring language

Match the creative to the intended audience: for example, Traditional Chinese for an appropriate Hong Kong audience, or Chinese and English variants where relevant.

A practical test sequence

  1. Research the market and verify the proposed locations.
  2. Review the available geographic and interest controls.
  3. Prepare appropriate product imagery and copy.
  4. Run a limited test.
  5. Compare qualified inquiries and orders, then refine the locations.

Geography is a segment to test; it does not establish an individual person’s budget or intention to buy.

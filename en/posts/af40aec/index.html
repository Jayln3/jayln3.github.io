LinkedIn B2B Marketing — From Account Setup to ABM

Created2026-04-19|Updated2026-04-19|Global Growth
|Post Views:
English edition, translated and edited from the Chinese article. Figures and product details refer to the original publication date. 中文原文

TL;DR: For B2B hardware, more traffic does not necessarily mean better inquiries. The original article focuses on LinkedIn’s professional targeting and account-based marketing: choosing target companies, then reaching the people involved in their purchasing decisions.

1. Why consider LinkedIn?

Smart-parking systems, access-control equipment and other infrastructure products are purchased by organizations. Their buying process may involve procurement, facilities and operations, with a long decision cycle and a substantial contract value.

The original article compares channels as follows. CPC ranges are its recorded examples, not current price quotations.

Platform Audience orientation Original CPC example
Meta / Facebook / Instagram Broad consumer audiences $0.50–2
TikTok Entertainment and discovery $0.30–1
Google Active search intent $2–8
LinkedIn Professional roles and companies $5–15

LinkedIn’s attraction in this context is the ability to work with professional attributes, such as procurement and facilities-management roles.

2. Three parts of the platform

Talent Solutions

The original article identifies recruitment as a major part of LinkedIn’s business. For a marketer, a company’s hiring activity may also provide context about its operations.

Marketing Solutions

Campaign Manager is the advertising interface. The article discusses:

  • Sponsored Content in the feed.
  • Sponsored Messaging.
  • Text Ads.
  • Video Ads.

Premium and Sales Navigator

Sales Navigator supports research by company, job role and industry, helping a team identify accounts and relevant people.

The proposed combination is to research companies and decision-makers, then coordinate the advertising approach around that account list.

3. Account-based marketing

ABM is a strategy, not a separate advertising system.

The broad-reach approach starts with a large audience and qualifies the resulting leads. The ABM approach starts with a list of target companies and works toward relevant people within those accounts.

Prepare the account list

The original workflow uses Matched Audiences in Campaign Manager with company-domain lists or customer-email lists.

Define the relevant roles

Combine the company list with the intended roles and industries:

  • Procurement managers.
  • Facilities directors.
  • Operations managers.
  • Senior executives.
  • Relevant sectors, such as property management, commercial real estate and manufacturing.

Match creative to the buying task

The original examples emphasize company-specific problems, minimum orders, customization and project references. The proposed destination is a case-study page and inquiry form.

Figures in the original article

Metric Broad campaign example ABM example
Impressions More than 100,000 5,000–20,000
Click-through rate 0.3–0.8% 1.5–3%
Inquiry quality Mixed More relevant business inquiries
Cost per acquisition $50–150 $80–200

The original article argues that a higher initial acquisition cost can still be worthwhile if lead quality and downstream conversion improve. It does not link a dataset for these figures.

4. Account setup and agency arrangements

Direct operation

The original checklist includes a personal LinkedIn account, Campaign Manager access and a suitable payment method.

The article raises a separate operational issue for a China-based business: how advertising payments and supporting documents are handled by its finance team.

Working with an agency

The article describes account setup, funding, campaign support and invoicing as possible agency services. It gives a 5–15% service-fee range as an example from the original publication.

Before agreeing terms, clarify:

  • The fee and how it is calculated.
  • Rebate conditions and timing.
  • Reporting frequency.
  • Whether campaign management and creative production are included.
  • Minimum funding requirements.
  • Which invoices and supporting documents will be supplied.

These are commercial questions to settle with the provider and the business’s finance team.

5. Choose channels around the purchase

The original article positions Meta and TikTok primarily for broad consumer reach, Google for active search demand, and LinkedIn for professional and company-level targeting.

Its illustrative decision framework is:

  • Consumer products: consider Meta or TikTok.
  • Lower-value B2B offers: test Google Ads alongside LinkedIn.
  • B2B orders around $1,000–10,000: investigate LinkedIn ABM.
  • Large or project-based contracts: combine ABM with deeper account research.

Illustrative budget allocation

Stage LinkedIn Meta
Initial testing 70% 30%
Scaling 50% 50%
Established brand and acquisition activity 40% 60%

These percentages are the original article’s example allocation for international B2B hardware marketing.

6. Execution priorities

  1. Define the intended companies, industries and roles.
  2. Build and maintain a target-account list.
  3. Match creative and landing pages to the purchasing problem.
  4. Agree operational and reporting responsibilities.
  5. Coordinate advertising with account research and follow-up.

The article’s central argument is that the relevance of a B2B inquiry can matter more than the raw number of clicks.
